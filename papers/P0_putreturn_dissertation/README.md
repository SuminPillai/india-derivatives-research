# Profitability of Delta-Neutral Option Strategies on Nifty 50 (2024 MBA Dissertation)

**SSRN:** https://ssrn.com/abstract=6747280
**Status:** Live

## Abstract

Equity index options are widely documented as overpriced relative to realized volatility, a
wedge commonly attributed to a priced volatility risk premium (VRP). This paper examines
whether systematic delta-neutral short-volatility strategies — specifically the short
straddle and short strangle — can capture that premium profitably on Indian equity
derivatives. Using monthly option contracts on Nifty 50 and nineteen constituent stocks over
the five-year period April 2019 to March 2024, this study constructs held-to-expiry payoffs
for four strategies — long straddle, short straddle, long strangle, and short strangle —
selected at delta-equivalent strikes. Statistical properties of the resulting return
distributions are characterized and subjected to Monte Carlo simulation (1,000 paths). The
results indicate that short-volatility strategies generate positive mean returns over the
sample period, while long-volatility strategies produce negative mean returns on average. At
the 95 percent confidence level, all four strategy distributions remain in positive territory
across the simulation paths. These findings are consistent with a persistent VRP in Indian
equity derivatives. The methodological limitations identified herein — particularly the
absence of transaction costs and the short sample horizon — motivate a more rigorous
follow-up (see P1 and P3 in this series).
